DMS v1.0 · Design Direction · Round 1

Modern. Restrained. Crafted.
Five directions to react to.

A pre-owned luxury car dealership has two user surfaces that should not look the same. The customer-facing surface must feel like a brand you'd trust with ₹80L. The internal staff surface must feel like a precision tool you'd trust with 50 deals a week. We pick a direction for each. We share one token system. That's how we get modern + great UX.

The two-surface frame

One brand. Two expressions.

Most DMS products fail at exactly this. They make internal tools too editorial (beautiful, slow, unusable at scale) or the customer surface too enterprise (feels cheap for the price tag). We keep shared brand primitives — typography family, color primitives, spacing scale, motion grammar, iconography — but density, chrome, and content treatment diverge.

Surface A

Customer-facing

Storefront · VDP · Customer portal · Consignor portal · CPO certificate view · Invoice share · Service booking · Delivery experience.

Qualities needed: editorial, emotional, photography-forward, aspirational, trust-building, conversion-aware.

Surface B

Internal staff

Sales CRM · Lead pipeline · Inventory management · RO board · Parts counter · Body shop · Finance · Admin · 24-role access controls.

Qualities needed: dense, keyboard-first, data-rich, scannable, fast, zero-animation theatre, dark-mode ready.

Recommendation

Pair 02 + 03. Keep 05 as a feature layer.

Customer surface gets Boutique E-commerce (02). Staff surface gets Modern Product Interface (03). We layer Dark Premium (05) into specific moments — top-tier collection vehicles, VIP delivery flows, launch pages — without letting it take over. Editorial Luxury (01) informs tone and voice but is too low-density to be a daily driver. Transactional Marketplace (04) is what we're deliberately not.

Customer surface

02 · Boutique E-commerce

Storefront, VDP, customer portal, consignor portal, invoice/certificate share.

  • Warm off-white paper + ink; single deep accent
  • Inter across the whole system; weight range 300-700
  • Editorial crops + clean spec tables (not hero-only)
  • Micro-motion: hover, focus, reveal
  • Mobile-first; 60% of luxury buyers browse on phone
  • Dark-mode optional, not required

Staff surface

03 · Modern Product Interface

Sales CRM, RO board, inventory, parts, body shop, finance, admin.

  • Near-white base + near-black ink; muted state palette
  • Inter UI + IBM Plex Mono for IDs and numbers
  • Dense tables, keyboard-first, command palette (⌘K)
  • Motion 80-160ms max; confirms, never decorates
  • Desktop-first; mobile as companion view for field roles
  • Dark mode is the default actually shipped

Both surfaces share: the same color primitives, the same type family (Inter), the same spacing scale, the same motion grammar, the same iconography. What diverges: density, chrome, content treatment, interaction vocabulary. That's the two-surface design system.

Before we lock this — where do you push back?

  1. Do you agree with the two-surface frame, or do you want one visual system across customer + staff (I'd argue against, but you know your team)?
  2. Is 02 Boutique E-commerce the right pick for customer surface, or does 05 Dark Premium feel closer to the brand you want?
  3. Is 03 Modern Product Interface right for staff, or do you want something more distinctive (risk: less off-the-shelf component support from shadcn)?
  4. Should 05 Dark Premium be reserved for top-tier "collection" vehicles only, or applied to the whole customer surface?
  5. Typography: Inter everywhere, or should customer surface use a serif display face (Playfair / Cormorant) for headlines?
  6. Photography: who shoots the cars? Studio-grade photography is non-negotiable for 02 or 05 to work. This is a cost line item we haven't discussed.
